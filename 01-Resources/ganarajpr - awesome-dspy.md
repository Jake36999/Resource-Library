# ganarajpr - awesome-dspy

## Bottom Line
A single 96-line Markdown file pointing at roughly sixty DSPy projects, articles, talks and papers — the fastest available survey of what people have built with DSPy, and not itself DSPy.

## What It Solves
- Answer “what has anyone actually done with this” faster than the library's own documentation can.
- Collect the talks and papers that explain the optimiser story in one place.
- Surface adjacent projects — STORM, xmc.dspy, dspy-redteam — that would be hard to find by search.

## Architecture & Mechanics
- One flat `README.md` with sections: Projects, Blogs/Articles, Twitter Threads, Newsletter, Videos, Tutorials, Papers.
- Maintained by pull request, with no visible inclusion bar and no annotation beyond a one-line description.
- Everything is an outbound link; nothing is vendored or archived, so the list decays exactly as fast as its targets do.

## What Is Inside
- **One file** — `README.md`, 96 lines, ~60 links across Projects, Blogs/Articles, Twitter
  Threads, a Newsletter, Videos, Tutorials and Papers.
- **What the Projects section actually points at** — STORM, `xmc.dspy` (extreme multi-label
  classification), `dspy-redteam`, DSPyGen, `dspy-inspector`, `dspy-rag-fastapi`, and
  instrumentation integrations (Arize Phoenix, OpenInference, Langtrace, Opik).
- **Nothing is vendored.** Every entry is an outbound link, so the list decays as its targets do.
- **Not here:** DSPy itself, which is `stanfordnlp/dspy`.

## Transferable Capability
**Survey what a thing has actually been used for, as distinct from what it is for.** A compilation of applications answers a question its subject's own documentation cannot: whether anybody has done something resembling what you are about to attempt, and what it cost them.

**Alternative to:** reading the documentation of a tool, which describes intent rather than use; and to searching the open web, which cannot distinguish a working application from an announcement. **Applies wherever** the decision is *should I adopt this* rather than *how does this work* — the evidence for that decision is other people's applications, and it is not held anywhere by default.

*Uncurated: entries are accepted without a visible bar, so read it as a survey of what exists rather than of what worked.*
