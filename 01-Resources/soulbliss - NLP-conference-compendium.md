# soulbliss - NLP-conference-compendium

## Bottom Line
A single README collecting the materials that come out of NLP conferences — tutorials, workshop proceedings, slides, videos — rather than the papers, which are the part everyone else indexes.

## What It Solves
- Find conference tutorials and workshop material, which are rarely indexed anywhere.
- Get an overview of a subfield from teaching material rather than from primary papers.
- Locate slides and recordings that are scattered across organiser websites.

## Architecture & Mechanics
- Two files. The entire artefact is `readme.md` plus one image, which is the honest minimum for a bibliography and is why 458 stars accrued to something with no infrastructure at all.
- The editorial choice is the whole contribution: indexing *tutorials and workshops* rather than papers, on the basis that a tutorial is a better entry point to an unfamiliar subfield than a paper is.
- **Not read:** `readme.md`. The scope above is the repository's stated purpose; individual entries were not verified.

## What Is Inside
- **One `readme.md`** — the compendium.
- **One image** under `img/`.
- **Two paths in total, 628 kB.** Last pushed 2024-02-22.
- **Not here:** any structure a machine could read, any licence, and anything after early 2024.

## Transferable Capability
**Index the material a field produces that nobody archives.** Papers get DOIs, citation graphs and search engines; tutorials, workshop proceedings, slides and recordings get a page on an organiser's site that disappears when the conference does. Collecting the *unarchived* layer is a real service precisely because the well-served layer needs no help — and it is a general observation about where curation is worth doing at all: **survey what is already indexed before building an index.**

The accompanying weakness is instructive for this catalogue's own design. A single flat README cannot be filtered, sorted or queried, so the collection is useful to a person who reads it once and nearly useless to anything programmatic — which is the difference between a list and a reference.

**Alternative to:** a paper search engine, which covers the well-covered layer; and to a structured dataset, which this is not. **Applies wherever** valuable material exists outside the channel that everyone indexes — internal talks, runbooks, post-mortems, design documents.
